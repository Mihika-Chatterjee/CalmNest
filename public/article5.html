It's okay to not be okay sometimes

You don’t always have to be strong, and that’s perfectly fine.

The Automatic Response

Often, when asked how we’re doing, the automatic response is “I’m fine.” While this may feel safe and neutral, it can sometimes hide the true state of our mental well-being. Even when life seems balanced with good sleep, healthy routines, and social connections, feelings of exhaustion, melancholy, or anxiety can still emerge.

Acknowledging Your Feelings

Acknowledging these feelings is an important step toward self-care. Avoiding or dismissing emotions may create a cycle of negativity that impacts physical health, academics, work, and relationships.

Seeking Support

Support can come from friends, loved ones, or mental health professionals. Therapy and counseling provide a safe space to explore feelings, recognize patterns, and develop strategies for self-compassion. Sharing emotions without fear of judgment helps individuals feel validated and more at peace with themselves.

Taking the First Step

Taking the first step to ask for help can bring relief that “powering through” never can. Being honest about your emotions, accepting them, and reaching out for support is a sign of strength. People around you care more than you may realize, and it is okay to let them in.

Prioritizing Mental Health

Prioritizing mental health is essential. Take the time to be kind to yourself, and remember: it’s your first time living — give yourself grace along the way.
